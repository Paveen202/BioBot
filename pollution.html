Many causes · connected effects

Pollution is more than littering

Smoke, dirty water, chemicals, noise, and excessive light can all pollute the environment. Some pollutants come from natural events, but human activities can create more, spread them, or make their effects last longer.

Poaching and overhunting harm nature too.They are usually classed as direct exploitation of wildlife, not pollution. Careless actions can cause pollution and other damage to habitats.

01 · Air

Air pollution

Sources: Vehicle exhaust, power plants, industry, cooking and heating fuels, and burning waste. Wildfires, dust storms, and volcanoes can also release smoke, particles, or gases.

Effects: Polluted air can harm people, wildlife, crops, forests, and climate. Some pollutants settle onto soil and water.

02 · Freshwater and oceans

Water pollution

Sources: Untreated sewage, industrial discharges, oil and fuel spills, mining waste, farm runoff, and litter carried by drains and rivers. Natural erosion can also add sediment.

Effects: Contaminants can poison aquatic life, damage habitats, and make water unsafe for people and animals.

03 · Land and soil

Soil pollution

Sources: Pesticides, excess fertilizers, mining, leaking fuel or chemicals, and poorly managed rubbish. Some harmful elements also occur naturally in rocks and soil.

Effects: Contaminated soil can harm plants and soil organisms, and some pollutants can move into groundwater or food chains.

04 · Waste

Plastic and solid-waste pollution

Sources: Single-use packaging, discarded fishing gear, clothing fibers, electronics, and waste that is not collected or safely managed.

Effects: Animals can become entangled in debris or mistake it for food. Plastic breaks into smaller pieces and can persist in ecosystems.

05 · Harmful substances

Chemical pollution

Sources: Some industrial chemicals, pesticides, solvents, medicines, and metals released through manufacturing, farming, mining, or unsafe disposal.

Effects: Certain chemicals can remain in the environment, harm organisms, or build up through food webs. Chemical pollution can affect air, water, and soil.

06 · Excess nutrients

Nutrient pollution

Sources: Too much nitrogen or phosphorus from fertilizers, manure, and wastewater washing into lakes, rivers, and coastal waters.

Effects: Excess nutrients can fuel harmful algal blooms. As blooms die and decompose, oxygen can fall and fish or other aquatic life may be harmed.

07 · Sound

Noise pollution

Sources: Road and air traffic, construction, ships, and some industrial activities. Storms and animals also make natural sounds; pollution is disruptive human-made noise.

Effects: Loud or persistent noise can interfere with wildlife communication, finding food, avoiding danger, and moving through habitats.

08 · Artificial light

Light pollution

Sources: Bright, misdirected, or unnecessary outdoor lighting from buildings, roads, signs, and sports areas.

Effects: Artificial light at night can confuse migrating birds and insects and disrupt natural day-and-night patterns for wildlife.

09 · Heat

Thermal pollution

Sources: Warm water released by some industrial processes, or loss of tree shade along streams and rivers.

Effects: Rapid changes in water temperature can stress aquatic species and reduce the oxygen available to them.

10 · Radioactive material

Radioactive contamination

Sources: Improperly managed radioactive waste, some mining and industrial processes, or accidents. Small amounts of naturally occurring radioactive materials are also found in rocks and soil.

Effects: If radioactive material is released, it can contaminate air, land, or water and require careful monitoring and cleanup.

Other human pressures on nature

Not all environmental harm is pollution

🐾

Poaching and illegal wildlife trade

Taking protected animals or plants from the wild can drive populations down and disrupt ecosystems.

🦌

Overhunting and overfishing

Removing wildlife faster than populations can recover can upset food webs and threaten species.

🌱

Careless actions

Leaving fishing line or rubbish, dumping chemicals, disturbing nests, or damaging habitat can hurt wildlife. Some actions cause pollution; others cause direct harm.

🌳

Habitat destruction

Clearing or fragmenting forests, wetlands, grasslands, and reefs removes shelter, food, and breeding places.

Small actions and bigger changes

How can we help?

  • Use less disposable packaging, reuse what you can, and sort waste according to local rules.
  • Never pour oil, paint, medicines, or chemicals down drains; use local safe-disposal programs.
  • Save energy and choose cleaner transport when available to reduce pollution from burning fuels.
  • Use garden chemicals carefully and follow local guidance on fertilizers and pesticides.
  • Keep outdoor lights pointed downward and switch them off when not needed.
  • Respect wildlife: keep a safe distance, do not buy products made from threatened species, and report suspected wildlife crime to local authorities.
  • Support clean water, wildlife protection, and responsible waste-management efforts in your community.
