Understand the risk · protect people and nature

Natural Hazards & Disaster Risk Reduction

Earthquakes, storms, floods, and droughts are natural hazards. We may not be able to stop them, but we can make disasters less likely and reduce the harm they cause.

A hazard is not automatically a disaster.Disaster risk rises when a hazard affects people or ecosystems that are exposed and vulnerable, especially when they have fewer ways to prepare or recover.

01 · Earth processes

Geological hazards

Earthquakes happen when stress along faults is released as the ground moves. Volcanoes erupt when magma and gases rise through the Earth's crust. Steep slopes can also fail as landslides, sometimes after heavy rain or shaking.

02 · Weather and water

Weather-related hazards

Storms, extreme heat, drought, and heavy rainfall can trigger floods and wildfires. Natural climate patterns shape these events, while human-caused climate change can worsen some climate-related hazards.

03 · People and places

Why impacts differ

Where homes are built, building strength, access to warnings, health, income, and emergency services all affect how severely people are harmed. Losing wetlands, forests, or other natural buffers can increase some local risks.

04 · Nature and wildlife

Why conservation matters

Healthy ecosystems can help communities cope with some hazards: wetlands can store floodwater, and coastal mangroves can reduce wave energy. Protecting habitat also gives wildlife more room and safer places during disturbance.

Reduce risk before a disaster

What can we do?

  • Use early-warning systems. Make sure alerts reach everyone and communities know what to do.
  • Build and plan safely. Use hazard maps, strong building standards, and land-use plans that avoid high-risk areas where possible.
  • Prepare together. Practice evacuation plans, identify safe places, and check on neighbors who may need help.
  • Protect natural buffers. Conserve and restore local forests, wetlands, dunes, and mangroves where they can reduce risk.
  • Reduce climate risks. Cut greenhouse-gas pollution and prepare for climate impacts such as heat, drought, and heavier rainfall.
  • Follow trusted local guidance. Emergency services and local authorities know the hazards and plans for your area.

The goal is disaster risk reduction: prevent new risks, reduce existing risks, and strengthen communities so they can respond and recover.
